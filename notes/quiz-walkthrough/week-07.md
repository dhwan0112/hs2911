### Q1 — Turing's replacement for "Can machines think?"  ·  Answer: E
**Q:** What did Turing (1950) propose instead of asking "Can machines think?"
**Why E:** A behavioural test (the Imitation Game): can a machine converse so that people cannot tell it from a human. It tests observable behaviour, not internal states. `[S12]`
**Why not the others:** A) legal test, invented · B) speed test, invented · C) maths test, invented · D) biological/pain test, invented.
**Trap:** invented construct

### Q2 — What ELIZA illustrates  ·  Answer: A
**Q:** What point does the lecture make with ELIZA (1966)?
**Why A:** ELIZA (Weizenbaum) is the example of people anthropomorphising a very simple pattern-matching chatbot. `[S13, S15]`
**Why not the others:** B) ELIZA was not trained on internet text · C) effect arose from simple text, not a humanlike voice · D) not built to deliver therapy with human-equal effects · E) ELIZA did not pass a Turing test proving thought.
**Trap:** anachronism / overclaim

### Q3 — Central claim of CASA  ·  Answer: E
**Q:** What is CASA's central claim (Nass, Steuer & Tauber, 1994)?
**Why E:** People apply social rules to computers automatically, even though they know computers have no feelings, selves or genders. Responses are not due to belief it is human, ignorance, dysfunction or a programmer's presence. `[S14, S15]`
**Why not the others:** A) mistaken belief it is human · B) inexperience (participants were experienced) · C) belief in a programmer behind replies · D) loneliness/dysfunction.
**Trap:** CASA explained by belief/deficit (the exact thing CASA rules out)

### Q4 — Point of the anthropomorphism timeline  ·  Answer: B
**Q:** Slide 15 places "anthropomorphism" (1668) with ELIZA, CASA and cartoon characters. What does it show?
**Why B:** Attributing humanlike traits to non-human things is an old, widespread tendency long predating AI. CASA used experienced users to show it persists. `[S15]`
**Why not the others:** A) word coined 1668, not for ELIZA/computers · C) tendency has not weakened with knowledge · D) not defined as belief in consciousness; term predates CASA · E) not limited to human-looking entities.
**Trap:** wrong history / narrowing the concept

### Q5 — CASA versus digital minds shift  ·  Answer: A
**Q:** What is the key shift between CASA (1990s) and "digital minds" (Anthis et al., 2025)?
**Why A:** CASA: simple computers triggering "mindless" social scripts (Reeves & Nass 1996). Digital minds: general-purpose systems with apparent reasoning, emotion and agency. `[S17]`
**Why not the others:** B) CASA was not about AI–AI groups · C) reversed · D) not the same claim, not robots vs computers · E) CASA did not claim computers have minds; digital minds does not claim no AI could.
**Trap:** reversed direction

### Q6 — Digital companionship framework grid  ·  Answer: D

**Why not the others:** A) swaps columns · B) availability and support are non-humanlike positives, not humanlike · C) customization/reset are non-humanlike positives · E) natural conversation/humor are humanlike.
**Trap:** swapped grid cells

### Q7 — Why chatbots differ in character (Booth)  ·  Answer: B

### Q8 — Morrin et al. on "AI psychosis" evidence  ·  Answer: B
**Q:** How does Morrin et al. (2025) characterise the evidence on "delusional spirals"/"AI psychosis"?

**Why not the others:** A) conclusive causation, overclaim · C) unrelated to design, contradicts engagement/affirmation point · D) reduces delusions, reversed · E) not a formal diagnosis.
