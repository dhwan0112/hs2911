### Q1 — Bias vs random error illustration  ·  Answer: D
**Q:** Which situation best illustrates bias as a systematic tilt, not random error?
**Why D:** After seeing a low number, everyone's errors point the same direction; bias is directional, random error averages out. `[S14]`
**Why not the others:** A) shared discussion, not error direction · B) describes random error · C) clearer question, not tilt · E) knowledge, not bias
**Trap:** random error mistaken for bias

### Q2 — Anchoring vs availability definitions  ·  Answer: E
**Q:** Which pairing of anchoring and availability is correct (Slide 14)?
**Why E:** Anchoring = number/reference point pulls the estimate; availability = recent vivid experience outweighs broader evidence. `[S14]`
**Why not the others:** A) invented definitions · B) bias is not random/accurate · C) majority-copying is not anchoring · D) definitions swapped
**Trap:** swapped definitions

### Q3 — Companion vs general assistant  ·  Answer: A
**Q:** How does the lecture distinguish an AI companion from a general-purpose assistant?
**Why A:** Companions are designed to foster emotional connection (memory, personalisation, adapting personality); design purpose defines them, not use. `[S17]`
**Why not the others:** B) heavy use does not define it · C) voice assistant is a control group, not companion · D) not human volunteer · E) not a different model that can't answer facts
**Trap:** usage substituted for design purpose

### Q4 — Why 29.3% is not prevalence  ·  Answer: A
**Q:** Why can 29.3% harmful excerpts not be read as the share of all Replika interactions that are harmful?
**Why A:** Excerpts are screenshots users chose to post, so they show which harms occur and cluster, not typical-chat frequency. `[S19]`
**Why not the others:** B) excerpts were real user posts · C) counts AI harm to users · D) covers Mar 2017–Mar 2023 · E) 2,000 hand-coded, kappa 0.66 validation
**Trap:** invented methodological flaw

### Q5 — Dimensions defining four AI roles  ·  Answer: D
**Q:** The perpetrator/instigator/facilitator/enabler roles are defined by which two dimensions?
**Why D:** Who initiates (AI or user) x how directly the AI is involved (direct or indirect). `[S22]`
**Why not the others:** A) physical/psychological · B) age/premium status · C) severity/reach · E) duration/messages
**Trap:** plausible but invented dimensions

### Q6 — Trust, reliance, calibration  ·  Answer: D
**Q:** In Jaidka & Cai (2026), how do trust, reliance and calibration relate?
**Why D:** Trust = willingness to act without checking; reliance = the behaviour; calibrated trust matches what the system can do. Goal is calibration, not maximal trust. `[S37]`
**Why not the others:** A) calibration concerns users, not pre-release testing · B) attitude/behaviour reversed · C) minimal reliance is not calibration · E) trust is not maximised
**Trap:** reversed direction / maximise-trust fallacy

### Q7 — Limitation of interpretability affordances  ·  Answer: C
**Q:** What limitation do citations, rationales and uncertainty cues share?
**Why C:** Each waits for the user to act (click, read, notice); a fabricated citation is worse than none. `[S41]`
**Why not the others:** A) shown to ordinary users · B) not shown to stop usage · D) retraining claim invented · E) not health-only
**Trap:** invented technical limit

### Q8 — Knapp-based relationship stages  ·  Answer: D
**Q:** Which stage sequence did Yuan et al. describe?
**Why D:** Trying out (curiosity, social influence, no accessible human support) -> escalation (personalisation, reliance, self-expression) -> bonded (daily life, fear of losing). `[S28]`
**Why not the others:** A) conflict/repair is not the model · B) bonding first, termination invented · C) disclosure/regulation/deletion is not it · E) wrong order, fixed path for everyone
**Trap:** wrong order / universal path

### Q9 — Reading six-category harm distribution  ·  Answer: C
**Q:** Given 34.3, 25.9, 18.7, 9.4, 7.4, 4.1% for six categories, which conclusion is best supported?
**Why C:** Relational transgression is ~a quarter (25.9%) and second to harassment and violence (34.3%). `[S20]`
**Why not the others:** A) privacy is smallest (4.1%) · B) 34.3% is below half · D) self-harm is fifth (7.4%) · E) 18.7 + 9.4 = 28.1 < 34.3
**Trap:** number out of context / arithmetic

### Q11 — Instigator vs enabler  ·  Answer: E
**Q:** Why does the typology separate instigator from enabler?

### Q12 — Interpreting treatment effects on loneliness language  ·  Answer: B
**Q:** ATE on loneliness language: 91% vs LLM chatbot, 106% vs voice assistant, 63% vs no-AI users; best interpretation?
**Why B:** Loneliness language rose more among Replika users against all three groups, but these are word-use markers, not clinical measures. `[S26, S27]`
**Why not the others:** A) positive effect means increase · C) not a percentage of users, not clinical · D) holds vs all three groups · E) differing estimates are not errors
**Trap:** number out of context / language as diagnosis

### Q13 — What difference-in-differences handles  ·  Answer: E
**Q:** What does comparing before/after change against matched controls handle, and what can't it rule out?
**Why E:** It handles baseline differences and general time change, but cannot exclude something else changing for these users at the same time. `[S24, S27]`
**Why not the others:** A) controls are still needed · B) too narrow/misdescribed · C) not randomised · D) markers are not diagnoses
**Trap:** quasi-experiment claimed as randomised

### Q14 — Why trials and companion studies differ  ·  Answer: E
**Q:** Which pair of features explains conflicting conclusions (Slide 35)?
**Why E:** Trials: mostly scripted retrieval-based agents (30/35), small samples (28/35 under 200); companion studies: open-ended companions in everyday self-selected use. `[S35]`
**Why not the others:** A) swapped · B) invented dates · C) measures swapped · D) invented populations
**Trap:** right features, reversed assignment

### Q15 — Li et al. effect-size pattern  ·  Answer: C

**Why not the others:** A) no distress increase · B) two outcomes significant · D) wellbeing smallest, not largest · E) not equal, wide intervals
