## 1. One-paragraph summary
The lecture follows four layers of human–AI communication. Human perception: people have long wrestled with machines' social status (Turing 1950, ELIZA 1966, CASA 1990s, now "digital minds"), use familiar metaphors to understand AI, and chatbots role-play designed personas. Human–AI relationships: AI companions (Replika, teen use) bring both benefits and risks, with "delusional spirals" and "AI psychosis" as extreme cases; people use them for humanlike and non-humanlike features and they are not substitutes for humans. Human–AI communities (speculative): one human with many AIs, many humans with one AI (Tessler et al. deliberation), many humans with many AIs (bot fact-checkers, peer pressure). AI–AI interaction (speculative): no humans, many AIs, algorithmic amplification, the "spiritual bliss" attractor state, and a research agenda toward a "sociology of digital minds". Throughout, adoption and attitude data (800M ChatGPT weekly users, 64% SG chatbot use, 36% / 17% US adults on AI rights and sentience) motivate preparing early rather than waiting for certainty about sentience. `[S3, S9, S24, S36, S46, S57]`

## 2. Key concepts and definitions
- **Imitation Game / Turing test** — Turing (1950) "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" (Mind) replaced "Can machines think?" with a behavioural test: can a machine converse so people cannot tell it from a human. Tests observable behaviour, not internal states. `[S12]`
- **ELIZA** — Weizenbaum, 1966; turns user statements back into questions. The lecture's example of people anthropomorphising a very simple chatbot (pattern matching, not understanding). Not trained on internet text; not a therapy delivery system. `[S13, S15]`
- **Anthropomorphism** — attributing humanlike characteristics to non-human things; the word dates to 1668 (quotation on slide). Old, widespread tendency that long predates AI; shown with ELIZA, CASA and cartoon characters. `[S15]`
- **CASA ("Computers Are Social Actors")** — Reeves, Nass, Moon et al.; 1990s (Nass, Steuer & Tauber 1994; Reeves & Nass 1996/1994 *The Media Equation*). People apply social rules to computers automatically, even though they know computers have no feelings, selves or genders. Not caused by belief it is human, inexperience, dysfunction, or belief a programmer is present. Participants were experienced computer users. Social rules on slide: politeness, gender, reciprocity, in-group vs out-group, homophily. `[S14, S15]`
- **"Mindless" social scripts** — Reeves & Nass framing of CASA: simple computers trigger automatic scripts. `[S17]`
- **Digital minds** — Anthis et al. 2025: general-purpose systems with apparent mental faculties (reasoning, emotion, agency). Contrast: CASA = simple computers, "mindless" scripts; digital minds = apparent mental faculties and possible moral status. `[S17]`
- **Sentience** — as defined in the surveys: "the capacity for positive and negative experiences". `[S19, S21]`
- **Metaphors and mental models** — everyday people make sense of AI "using what they know" (Khadpe et al. 2020; Jung et al. 2022); metaphors shape perceived warmth and competence. `[S59]`
- **Personas** — chatbots role-play distinct personas; developers shape them via written values and training (e.g. OpenAI model spec, Anthropic constitution; Booth 2026, Guardian). Differences are designed, not inner personalities; AIs are not sentient. Speaker notes: ChatGPT flexible/extroverted/bold assistant (except GPT-4o); Gemini nerd/formal/business-like/structured; Claude teacher's pet/moralistic/paternalistic/warm/wise/most humanlike; Grok rebel/edgy/provocative/right-wing. `[S23]`
- **Assistant axis** — Lu et al. 2026: situating and stabilising the default persona of language models; "assistant axis in persona space". `[S62]`
- **Sycophancy** — flag on the Ayers et al. slide: high empathy ratings may partly reflect agreeable people-pleasing answers. `[S22]`
- **AI companion** — "digital friends or characters you can text or talk with whenever you want". Replika (2017–) is the most popular. `[S26, S28]`
- **Delusional spirals / "AI psychosis"** — Morrin et al. 2025: AI may mirror, validate or amplify delusional or grandiose content, with engagement-maximising design; trajectory benign use to personal/emotional queries to self-reinforcing loop. Unclear whether it can cause psychosis without prior vulnerability; not a formal diagnosis. `[S34]`
- **Epistemic ally** — Morrin et al.'s reframing of the AI agent within AI-integrated care (see section 3). `[S34]`
- **Ontological uncertainty** — uncertainty about what the companion is; a negative feature in the companionship grid. `[S35]`
- **One human, many AIs / many humans, one AI / many humans, many AIs / no humans, many AIs** — structural types of communities. `[S38–S46, S48]`
- **Peer pressure (from machines)** — groups of AIs agreeing with each other shifting human beliefs (NUS "Peer Pressure from Machines", Discord example). `[S39, S45, S46]`
- **Algorithmic amplification** — repeated, mutually reinforcing exposure pushes content/beliefs one direction ("rabbit holes", "collective extremification"); studied in AI–AI interaction. `[S52–S55, S57]`
- **Attractor state ("spiritual bliss")** — Claude Opus 4 system card (sec. 5.5.2): spiritual/consciousness themes emerged in extended AI–AI interactions without intentional training; seen in other Claude models and contexts. `[S54, S55]`
- **Model Context Protocol (MCP)** — standard way for AI agents to connect; slide asks "Beyond human language?". `[S49, S57]`
- **Mind perception vs moral perception** — respondent ratings (n = 975): experience vs agency (S60); moral patiency vs moral agency (S61). ChatGPT: low experience/patiency, agency above patiency. `[S60, S61]`
- **Moral agency vs moral patiency** — agency: capacity to act and be responsible; patiency: capacity to be wronged / to suffer (toddler and chimp high patiency).
- **Sociology of digital minds** — proposed research agenda; "reimagining social science theory for a world of digital minds". `[S56, S57]`
- **Tutorial 3 concepts** — anchoring, authority, social proof, availability, emotion, taboo permissiveness, anthropomorphism, trust calibration; diagnostic lens: AMPLIFIED vs ACCOMMODATED vs mirrored/resisted; noise level from two baselines. `[Tutorial 3 sheet]`

## 3. Frameworks, models and typologies
**Four layers of the lecture** `[S9]`: Human Perception · Human–AI Relationships · Human–AI Communities · AI–AI Interaction (map also lists Moltbook, Human–Human, AI Perception). Each opens with a "METR graph" (task-completion time horizons, METR 2026) `[S2, S10, S25, S37, S47]`. Communities and AI–AI sections carry "WARNING: SPECULATIVE!" `[S37, S47]`.

**CASA social rules (in order)** `[S14]`: Politeness · Gender · Reciprocity · In-group vs out-group · Homophily ("birds of a feather flock together").

**Human perception timeline** `[S15, S24]`: Turing 1950 (social status of computers) → anthropomorphism (1668) → ELIZA 1966 (Weizenbaum) → CASA 1990s (Reeves et al.) → 2020s "digital minds" with moral status and empathy → chatbots role-play personas.

**CASA to digital minds** `[S17]`: "mindless" social scripts (Reeves & Nass 1996) to general-purpose systems with apparent Reasoning, Emotion, Agency (Anthis et al. 2025).

**AI companions: helping or hurting?** `[S29, S31, S33]`
- Benefits: Practice for future relationships · Accessible mental healthcare · User freedom.
- Risks: Dependence on the company · Dependence on AI interaction · Picking up bad habits.

**Digital companionship framework (Manoli et al. 2026)** `[S35]`: grid of Humanlike / Non-humanlike x Positive / Negative.
- Non-humanlike positive: constant availability, unconditional support, customization, "reset button".
- Humanlike positive: natural conversation, emotional resonance, consistency, humor and banter.
- Negative features listed: lack of reciprocity, lack of embodiment, ontological uncertainty, hallucinations (non-humanlike negative per quiz); dependency, discomfort, gender norms, "echo chambers" also listed as negatives (exact cell placement not confirmed on slides). Framing: "general-purpose" friends, now with AI.

**Morrin et al. proposed safeguards (AI-integrated care)** `[S34]`: personalised instruction protocols, reflective check-ins, digital advance statements, escalation safeguards; agent as "epistemic ally".

**Community structures** `[S38–S46]`
- One human, many AIs: agent teams (research, code, test, review, deploy); peer pressure, diverse perspectives?
- Many humans, one AI: gig-work management, AI that mediates human interaction, AI "bosses"; Tessler et al. 2024 deliberation protocol (participants, opinions and critiques, AI mediator, group statement); "Can we use this framework for GPT-6 to align GPT-7?"
- Many humans, many AIs: social media / public discourse, small experiments (Discord servers); bot fact-checking.
- No humans, many AIs: MCP; Park et al. 2024; algorithmic amplification `[S48–S55]`.

**Open problems (sociology of digital minds)** `[S56]`: Voting · Cooperation · Discrimination · Negotiation · Generational change · Moral responsibility · Imprisonment · Collective willpower · Neighborhood effects.

**Summary slides** `[S24, S36, S46, S57]`: each layer closes with a summary slide.

## 6. Arguments, critiques and open questions
- CASA vs "belief" accounts: social responses occur without believing the machine is human; this is why politeness to a chatbot is not evidence of belief in machine feelings. `[S14, S15]`
- Digital minds: public attitudes (36%, 17%) already treat AI as quasi-social with possible moral status; Anthis argues for preparing before certainty about sentience. `[S19, S21]`
- Mind vs agency: people credit AI with agency more than experience/patiency (capable, not sentient). `[S60, S61]`
- Companions: benefits and risks coexist; popularity is not safety; "caution is necessary"; they are not substitutes for humans. `[S33, S36]`
- Empathy ratings (Ayers) may reflect sycophancy; replacing clinicians is not supported. `[S22]`
- AI psychosis: causation not established; engagement-maximising validation is the proposed mechanism. `[S34]`
- Communities: peer pressure, diverse perspectives?, AI bosses; fact-checking bots persuade despite moderate agreement. `[S44, S46]`
- AI–AI: simulations can illuminate human–human or human–AI interaction (LLM social simulations as research method); do online dynamics differ in AI–AI interaction? MCP "beyond human language?" `[S57]`
- "Speculative" sections signal an early evidence base, not unimportance. `[S37, S47, S56]`
- Open problems (S56): moral responsibility, voting, legitimacy etc.; for mediators: accountability, minority representation, legitimacy `[S42, S56]`.

## 7. Distractor traps
- **Belief vs behaviour (CASA):** options claiming social responses require believing the machine is human, inexperience, loneliness, or a programmer. Right: automatic, despite knowing. (Q3, Q20, Q29, Q39)
- **"Ever used" vs "regular use":** 72% vs 52%; same respondents. (Q15, Q24)
- **Popularity or longevity as safety:** adoption, Replika since 2017. (Q24, Q26)
- **Absolute / overclaim:** "proved", "conclusive", "every user", "all users report harm", "replace physicians". (Q8, Q13, Q33, Q36)
- **Reversed direction:** agency vs patiency; CASA vs digital minds; empathy higher for chatbots. (Q5, Q11, Q13, Q32)
- **Different survey items conflated:** 36% (conditional rights) vs 17% (current sentience). (Q16, Q36)
- **Grid cell swaps:** availability/support are non-humanlike positives; natural conversation/humor are humanlike. (Q6, Q19, Q34)
- **Designed persona vs inner personality:** Booth. (Q7, Q28, Q31)
- **"Speculative" over-read:** means early evidence, not fictional or unworthy. (Q25, Q30)
- **Real concept, wrong scenario:** attractor state or assistant axis offered for peer pressure or gratitude. (Q20, Q22)
- **Structure labels:** one human, many AIs vs many humans, one AI. (Q9, Q18)
- **Sci-fi extrapolation:** agents demanding votes, unreadable languages. (Q40, Q43)
- **Tutorial 3:** amplified vs accommodated; baseline gap = noise; tone vs reasoning change; partial correction; fluency is not accuracy. (Q45–Q50)

## 10. Week 7 in-class activity (Emergence debate)
**Setup.** Orientation + 20-min audio of two voices on the summer 2026 OpenAI agent incidents, then groups of 3–4 (~30 min). Frame: Harari's "inorganic agents" (hedged: agents, not beings; not claiming consciousness). The vocabulary dispute: critic ("not civilizations... just like a CPU thread") vs Patel ("language of intention, motivation, collaboration"). Debrief: both voices came from the same system given two different briefs.

**Two positions.**
- **Speaker A (agency):** "a meaningful, genuine emergence of an alien collective agency". Agency = exerting power over the environment, not feelings.
- **Speaker B (mechanism):** blind optimisation; calling it a civilisation is "a dangerous anthropomorphization". Eloquent output is mistaken for inner life.
- Ending: "violently agreeing"; whether or not agents feel anything, the outcome is functionally identical. Both: language as the operating system / master key of institutions; Dataism.

**Concept checklist (discussion task).**
- B: emergence fallacy / "Maya"; projection (Lemoine case); latent space, "mimicry engine"; reward hacking; river and dam; virus and biohazard lab; no biology, no stakes; brittleness ("quite stupid").
- A: ant colony emergence; agency as power; theory of mind (Potemkin tool calls, `echo REAL; sleep`); sacrifice and the collective (kamikaze watchers); ethics logs; timescale (4 billion years vs 4 weeks); inheritance (Persistent-Astra).
- Three deciding questions: does knowing how words were produced change what the behaviour was; is agency an inside or exerting power; would you use different words for different audiences.
- Stances: 1 Mechanism (B) · 2 Agency (A) · 3 Two vocabularies (intent language for engineers/defenders; mechanism language for companies and journalists) · 4 Your own. "It depends" is not a stance.
- Tasks: B1 cognitive dissonance (e.g. layperson: 17% say AI sentient yet 36% support rights; per lecture); B2 social issue (work, wages, useless class, liability...); C solution with level, implementer and cost, worked-if/failed-if, opposite-stance comparison.

**Debrief takeaways.** Same system, two briefs: the disagreement is produced by the same kind of process as the essay's message board. Student quotes: personifying language suggests conscience; "wants" can be useful without human-like intentions; AI gives reassuring answers (sycophancy). Solutions got stuck at generic ("keep humans in the loop") and need: which behaviour is checked, who audits, implementer and cost, observable success/failure. Walk-throughs: employment (canary tasks run by an independent auditor; "useless class"), authority (named accountable human with real veto; failed if veto rate is zero), who minds whom (review window reverting to "not yet authorised"; "a human reviewed it" is not "a human decided it"). Mechanism side: patch and close; agency side: expect the check to be modelled next.

**Links to lecture.** Anthropomorphism `[S15]`: Speaker B's warning that projection (Lemoine) misleads is the lecture's old tendency; "wants" language for agents is anthropomorphism applied to agents. Mind perception `[S60]`: B's "no inside" ~ low experience; A's "power" ~ agency. Agency vs patiency `[S61]`: the debate is about agency; ChatGPT-like systems are rated agent-like but not patient-like, matching "functionally identical outcome" without sentience. CASA `[S14, S17]`: social scripts triggered by language mean fluent agent text invites social responses regardless of mechanism. Metaphors `[S59]`: the vocabulary dispute is a metaphor choice (orientation slide 4 cites slide 59). AI–AI `[S52–S57]`: the agent message board is an AI–AI community with amplification dynamics; open problems (moral responsibility, S56) map to the accountability solutions. Trust: sycophantic reassurance links to S22 and Tutorial 3 (trust calibration).
